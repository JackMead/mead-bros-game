# Chapter 1: A Young Man in Whitby

**Author:** Rob
**Era and place:** August 1890, Whitby: the East Cliff, the old town and harbour, the swing bridge and the West Cliff
**Spotlight brother(s):** Phil, alone

## What happens

Phil lands in Whitby in the summer of 1890, the same weeks Bram Stoker is there on holiday gathering ideas for a vampire novel. He has no idea what's happened, and his way of coping is bravado: everything is fine, and everyone, whatever their age, is "young man".

He arrives at the Abbey ruins and St Mary's churchyard on the East Cliff. Then he has to get by in the working old town (the harbour, Church Street, smokehouses, jet workshops, fishwives, gulls) with no money, the wrong clothes and freezing hands, in August. His way up is across the bridge to the West Cliff, where Whitby's polite society stays, plays parlour games and dresses for dinner. Phil fits in there better than he should, because he's been getting quietly posher for years.

Along the way he works out where and when he is, and learns that the Petrifying Well in Knaresborough is already a famous attraction in 1890. That's the first sign of how the curse might be undone.

It ends with Phil safe for now and thinking of his brothers, wondering how Jack is getting on, wherever and whenever he is.

## Picks up from

- The curse, and the three streaks of light leaving the well.
- Phil's phone is the one left on the gravel by the well, so he's here without it.
- Phil's table tennis, not used in a puzzle yet.

## Leaves open

- **Chapter 2 is Jack's.** The ending turns to him, and where and when he landed is for the next author to decide.
- Phil's situation in 1890: he's getting by, but he isn't home.
- The Petrifying Well as the link between times: Phil now knows it matters, but not how to use it.

## Jokes, people, things to include

- **Phil's catchphrase is "young man"**, said to everyone, especially people much older than him. Use it heavily.
- **Bravado:** he pretends everything is fine, all the time.
- **Getting posher with age and money.** He's more at home on the West Cliff than in the old town, and more at home than he should be.
- **Engaged, with a wedding coming up.** He's worried about missing it. Keep his fiancée unnamed.
- **Works in cyber security but isn't technical.** Plenty of room for him to sound expert and be no help at all.
- **Table tennis:** genuinely very good and competitive, but friendly. No trash talk, but you only have to ask once and he'll tell you about beating Rob, Jack and Dad. Table tennis is a brand-new parlour craze in 1890.
- **Incredibly picky eater.** The running question is whether he'll eat it. He's in a fishing town.
- **Collectible monster trading cards and the phone game where you catch them.** He has cards on him, is gutted he can't play the game, and can't buy new packs in 1890.
- **Pop punk.** He's worried about missing his big summer pop-punk festival in Leeds this year.
- **Football:** a big Manchester fan (keep the club name generic), but he likes all sport.
- **What he carries:** trading cards and a table tennis bat, plus anything else Claude finds useful. Not his phone.
- **Bram Stoker appears**, and there should be lots of Dracula moments: the 199 steps, the churchyard, bats, a shipwreck, a mysterious black dog, and so on.
- **Real Whitby:** Whitby jet and mourning jewellery, kippers, herring, the swing bridge, the whalebone arch, the class split between East and West.
- **Brands:** keep the trading cards, the phone game, the football club and the festival generic. No real brand or character names in the game.

## Puzzle settings

- **Number of puzzles:** 4 to 5
- **Difficulty:** Medium, with a gentle opening
- **Length:** 20 to 30 minutes
- **Types you'd like:** a mix. The new things to learn are moving between places, clues spread across scenes, and more than one thing on the go at once.
- **Must use:** Phil's table tennis. At least one of his other traits.
- **Avoid:** re-teaching what the prologue already taught.
- **Scenes (four, one painted background each):** the East Cliff (Abbey ruins and churchyard), Church Street and the harbour, the swing bridge, and a West Cliff grand hotel parlour or terrace.
